Key figures

  • 36.3%

    of gendered words in national textbooks refer to women and girls.

  • 1,968

    national textbooks: 1,213 from the 2024 paper and 755 found since.

  • 66

    countries, 32 of them added since the paper.

Across 1,968 national textbooks from 66 countries, 36.3% of gendered words refer to women and girls: 1.8 male words for every female word. Among the 41 countries with at least 10 national textbooks in the model, the adjusted share runs from 26.4% in Maldives (95% interval 19.1–33.8) to 67.7% in Zimbabwe (58.3–77.2); intervals are wide where books are few. Book by book, words about children are 40.0% female and words about adults 41.5% (a difference of −1.4 points, 95% interval −3.0 to +0.1), so the data cannot rule out no difference. Among the 1,113 books published from 2000 on, the female share changes by +0.6 points a decade within countries (95% interval −3.4 to +4.7), so the data cannot rule out no change. Across countries, the adjusted female share shows no clear link with income (correlation −0.02).

Gendered words are nouns and pronouns such as she, mother and king; national textbooks are official English student textbooks. The figures apply the paper’s method to more books, so they differ from the published paper: see Methods.

Last updated 9 October 2026. The numbers are rebuilt as new books are scored.
