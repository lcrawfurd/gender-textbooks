In the national textbooks of the 97 countries coded so far, women and girls are 40.6% of the people pictured (see Pictures).

Across 3,226 national textbooks from 78 countries, 34.9% of gendered words refer to women and girls: 1.9 male words for every female word. Among the 41 countries with at least 10 national textbooks in the model, the adjusted share runs from 28.5% in Maldives (95% interval 21.0–35.9) to 68.8% in Zimbabwe (58.9–78.7); intervals are wide where books are few. Book by book, words about children are 38.2% female and words about adults 41.1% (a difference of −2.9 points, 95% interval −4.2 to −1.6). Among the 1,677 books published from 2000 on, the female share changes by −0.1 points a decade within countries (95% interval −2.6 to +2.5), so the data cannot rule out no change. Across countries, the adjusted female share shows no clear link with income (correlation −0.03). Women are 28.8% of the people national textbooks show in a job, against 37.5% of the same jobs in the labour market (see Stereotypes).

Gendered words are nouns and pronouns such as she, mother and king; national textbooks are official English student textbooks. The figures apply the paper’s method to more books, so they differ from the published paper: see Methods.

Last updated 12 November 2026. The numbers are rebuilt as new books are scored.

Pictures: who textbooks show

A model looked at random pages of each national textbook and recorded every person pictured: woman or man, child or adult, and what they are shown doing. It reads books in any language, not only English. 97 countries are coded so far, 20 random pages of each national textbook (30 in Pakistan and Rwanda, the first countries coded).
