Jobs

The jobs people are shown in, in Ethiopia’s national textbooks. Women are 35.9% of the people shown in a job that can be matched to labour-market data, against 33.4% of the same jobs in Ethiopia’s labour market (ILOSTAT): a gap of +2.5 points (95% interval −5.7 to +9.3).

The 5 jobs most often mentioned with a woman or a man (at least 10 mentions each), highest female share first; each bar splits a job’s mentions into women (left) and men (right). A mention is a job title next to a gendered word, as in the 2024 paper; only titles matched to a labour-market job group count. Labour-market figures exist only for broad job groups (a nurse and a priest fall in the same one), so they are compared with the books over all jobs together, above, not job by job.

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length. The pictures come from a model (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) that coded 20 random pages of each national textbook, in any language, following a written coding manual (pages a first look found no person on were not coded further); second copies of a book are counted once; the model’s disability flags are not yet checked by eye, so no disability figures are shown.
