United States

Gender in the United States’ school textbooks: what the living paper finds in its national textbooks, in their words. Figures as of 9 November 2026.

Words

How often women and girls are mentioned in the United States’ 474 English national textbooks. Adjusted for subject, grade and book length (the paper’s model), the United States’ share is the 14th lowest of the 41 countries ranked.

The female share is female words over all gendered words in those books (nouns and pronouns such as she, mother and king), pooled, with no adjustment. National textbooks are official English student textbooks. A share is shown only for a row with at least 200 gendered words; under that, a dash.

By state

Each state’s female share of gendered words in the United States’ national textbooks, adjusted for subject, grade and book length.

Each dot is a state’s adjusted share and each line its 95% interval; books with no state form a group of their own. Most of the 474 United States books (378) come from the state-made materials of Texas and Utah. Utah has books in one subject group only (Maths), so its figure assumes that the subject differences seen elsewhere in the United States hold there too.

Each state’s adjusted female share of gendered words, on a map.

Colour shows each state’s adjusted female share, as in the chart above, for states with at least 10 national textbooks in the model. The colour runs from amber below parity (more men) to teal above it (more women), deepest at 25% and 75% and beyond and palest at 50%. Grey: no estimate. Books with no state (No state recorded in the chart) are not on the map. Alaska and Hawaii, which have no books, are not drawn.

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length.
