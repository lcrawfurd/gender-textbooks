Syrian Arab Republic

Gender in the Syrian Arab Republic’s school textbooks: what the site finds in its national textbooks, in their words and in their pictures. Figures as of 26 November 2026.

Words

How often women and girls are mentioned in the Syrian Arab Republic’s 24 English national textbooks. Adjusted for subject, grade and book length (the paper’s model), the Syrian Arab Republic’s share is the 16th highest of the 41 countries ranked.

  • 42.6%

    of gendered words refer to women and girls, adjusted for subject, grade and book length (95% interval 35.5–49.6).

  • 40.5%

    of gendered words in all its national textbooks refer to women and girls (pooled, no adjustment).

  • 24

    national textbooks scored, added since the 2024 paper.

By subject

Only one subject group, Language, has at least 200 gendered words in the Syrian Arab Republic’s national textbooks: 40.5% of them are female, over 24 books.

Jobs

The jobs people are shown in, in the Syrian Arab Republic’s national textbooks. Women are 37.5% of the people shown in a job that can be matched to labour-market data, against 54.3% of the same jobs in the Syrian Arab Republic’s labour market (ILOSTAT): a gap of −16.8 points (95% interval −26.2 to −8.4).

The 3 jobs most often mentioned with a woman or a man (at least 10 mentions each), highest female share first; each bar splits a job’s mentions into women (left) and men (right). A mention is a job title next to a gendered word, as in the 2024 paper; only titles matched to a labour-market job group count. Labour-market figures exist only for broad job groups (a nurse and a priest fall in the same one), so they are compared with the books over all jobs together, above, not job by job.

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length. The pictures come from a model (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) that coded 20 random pages of each national textbook, in any language, following a written coding manual (pages a first look found no person on were not coded further); second copies of a book are counted once; the model’s disability flags are not yet checked by eye, so no disability figures are shown.
