Key figures

  • 34.9%

    of gendered words in national textbooks refer to women and girls.

  • 3,157

    national textbooks: 1,210 from the 2024 paper and 1,548 found since.

  • 78

    countries, 44 of them added since the paper.

Across 3,157 national textbooks from 78 countries, 34.9% of gendered words refer to women and girls: 1.9 male words for every female word. Among the 41 countries with at least 10 national textbooks in the model, the adjusted share runs from 28.5% in Maldives (95% interval 21.0–35.9) to 68.8% in Zimbabwe (58.9–78.7); intervals are wide where books are few. Book by book, words about children are 38.3% female and words about adults 41.2% (a difference of −2.9 points, 95% interval −4.2 to −1.6). Among the 1,677 books published from 2000 on, the female share changes by −0.1 points a decade within countries (95% interval −2.6 to +2.5), so the data cannot rule out no change. Across countries, the adjusted female share shows no clear link with income (correlation −0.03). Women are 28.7% of the people national textbooks show in a job, against 37.6% of the same jobs in the labour market (see Stereotypes).

Gendered words are nouns and pronouns such as she, mother and king; national textbooks are official English student textbooks. The figures apply the paper’s method to more books, so they differ from the published paper: see Methods.

Last updated 6 November 2026. The numbers are rebuilt as new books are scored.

Can children read their textbooks?

How far the textbooks for Grades 1–6 sit above the text that children are tested on (a Grade 2 story; for South Africa, PIRLS’s Grade 4 passages), in the language children are tested in, in 13 countries. A child who cannot read the test text cannot read a harder book, so the share who can read it is a ceiling on the share who can read their textbooks.

  • In Uganda, 7% of children in Grade 3 can read a Grade 2 story (Uwezo, 2024), and 16 of its 18 Grade 1–5 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 7% can read the harder ones in Grade 3 (4 textbooks), 22% in Grade 4 (3 textbooks), 36% in Grade 5 (5 textbooks) and 53% in Grade 6 (4 textbooks). Taken together, at most 38% (90% interval 34%–54%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Pakistan, 10% of children in Grade 3 can read simple English sentences (ASER, 2025; provisional report), and 23 of its 31 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 10% can read the harder ones in Grade 3 (2 textbooks), 16% in Grade 4 (2 textbooks), 54% in Grade 5 (6 textbooks), 54% in Grade 6 (6 textbooks) and 66% in Grade 7 (7 textbooks). Taken together, at most 63% (90% interval 58%–70%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In India, 10% of children in Grade 3 can read simple English sentences (ASER, 2022; rural children only), and all of its 238 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 6% can read the harder ones in Grade 2 (34 textbooks), 10% in Grade 3 (27 textbooks), 17% in Grade 4 (49 textbooks), 24% in Grade 5 (41 textbooks), 32% in Grade 6 (35 textbooks) and 40% in Grade 7 (52 textbooks). Taken together, at most 23% (90% interval 23%–25%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Tanzania, 15% of children in Grade 3 can read a Grade 2 story (Uwezo, 2017), and 6 of its 13 Grade 1–5 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 15% can read the harder ones in Grade 3 (1 textbook), 33% in Grade 5 (3 textbooks) and 40% in Grade 6 (2 textbooks). Taken together, at most 69% (90% interval 55%–80%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Malawi, of the children in Grade 3 given the English Grade 2 story, 16% could read it (MICS6, 2019), and 2 of its 5 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 14% can read the harder ones in Grade 5 (1 textbook) and 22% in Grade 7 (1 textbook). Taken together, at most 67% (90% interval 49%–81%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Kiribati, of the children in Grade 5 given the English Grade 2 story, 26% could read it (MICS6, 2018), and 15 of its 16 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 26% can read the harder ones in Grade 5 (3 textbooks), 22% in Grade 6 (6 textbooks) and 40% in Grade 7 (6 textbooks). Taken together, at most 35% (90% interval 31%–51%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Bangladesh (Bangla), 33% of children in Grade 3 can read a Grade 2 story in Bangla or English (MICS6, 2019), and 36 of its 47 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 33% can read the harder ones in Grade 3 (1 textbook), 45% in Grade 4 (7 textbooks), 64% in Grade 5 (7 textbooks), 74% in Grade 6 (7 textbooks) and 80% in Grade 7 (14 textbooks). Taken together, at most 75% (90% interval 72%–79%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Nigeria, of the children in Grade 3 given the English Grade 2 story, 38% could read it (MICS6, 2021), and 3 of its 11 Grade 1–3 textbooks are harder than that: at most 83% (90% interval 66%–91%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Guyana, of the children in Grade 3 given the English Grade 2 story, 75% could read it (MICS6, 2019), and 21 of its 35 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 51% can read the harder ones in Grade 2 (3 textbooks), 75% in Grade 3 (1 textbook), 79% in Grade 4 (4 textbooks), 65% in Grade 5 (5 textbooks), 62% in Grade 6 (4 textbooks) and 72% in Grade 7 (4 textbooks). Taken together, at most 80% (90% interval 76%–85%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.

For books in a language fewer of its children were tested in:

  • In Rwanda (English), 0% of children in Grade 6 can read a Grade 2 story (EGRA, 2011; 2011, before the 2015 curriculum; tested at the start of P6), and 2 of its 49 Grade 1–5 textbooks are harder than that: at most 96% (90% interval 89%–99%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In South Sudan (English), 2% of children in Grade 3 can read a Grade 2 story (EGRA, 2016; 25 chosen schools; the MICS story stands in for the unprinted passage), and 2 of its 10 Grade 1–2 textbooks are harder than that: at most 80% (90% interval 56%–94%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.
  • In Bangladesh (English), 33% of children in Grade 3 can read a Grade 2 story in Bangla or English (MICS6, 2019), and 24 of its 29 Grade 1–6 textbooks are harder than that. Against each grade’s figure, at most 64% can read the harder ones in Grade 5 (5 textbooks), 74% in Grade 6 (5 textbooks) and 80% in Grade 7 (14 textbooks). Taken together, at most 80% (90% interval 78%–84%) can read a textbook drawn at random from them.

Kenya: 40% of children read the Uwezo Grade 2 English story; in Glewwe, Kremer and Moulin’s schools (2009), the median pupil could read the English textbook in 16% of Grade 3 classes and 28% of Grade 4.
