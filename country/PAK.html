Skin tone

The skin tone of the people pictured, judged from the face alone, in photos and in drawings and for women and men, Pakistan.

Each bar splits the people in a group whose skin tone could be judged into three bands, lighter, medium and darker, from left to right; the count after each group is how many people were judged, and the share in the darker band is at the right. The model judged tone by eye from the face alone, cut out of the picture with nothing else of the page in view. Only people whose face could be cut out clearly are included: 1,759 of the 5,223 people pictured. The 183 people whose tone could not be judged (black-and-white pictures among them) are left out. Photos and drawings are shown apart, since a drawing’s colours are the illustrator’s choice. Groups with fewer than 30 people judged are not shown. Pakistan, 367 national textbooks, 30 random pages each (10,984 pages, 2,135 with a picture); second copies of a book are counted once.

Judged by eye with the whole page visible, the same people were put in the same band as from the face alone for 1,154 of 1,502 people (76.8%). The page changed some judgements: of the 205 people shown doing domestic work, 25.9% were judged lighter with the page visible, against 41.0% from the face alone.

Another measure, from the colour of the skin pixels of each face (the individual typology angle, after Adukia et al. 2023), was possible for 1,389 people. It also finds the people in drawings lighter than those in photos (69.5% of 718 in drawings in the lighter band, against 37.9% of 535 in photos). It is not charted: shadows and faded or sepia photos darken faces, so it is unreliable at the darker end (see Methods).

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length. The pictures come from a model (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) that coded 30 random pages of each national textbook, in any language, following a written coding manual (pages a first look found no person on were not coded further); second copies of a book are counted once, and every disability flag was checked by eye.
