How women and men are described

The adjectives used of women and men (“a kind woman”, “he seemed strong”) and the verbs they are the subject of (“she cooked”, “he built”), scored on the NRC lexicon from 0 to 1. Dominance stands in for agency, as in the paper. In dominance, the adjectives used of women differ from those used of men by −0.019 (95% interval −0.028 to −0.009); the verbs women are the subject of differ from those of men by −0.011 (95% interval −0.014 to −0.005). Dots are the gaps and lines their 95% intervals: teal where women’s words score higher, amber where men’s do, and grey where the interval holds no gap.

Countries cannot be told apart on these measures: two countries would need to differ by at least 0.119 in adjective dominance, 6 times the gap across all books, so no country values are shown.
