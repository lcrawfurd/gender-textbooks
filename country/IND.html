India

Gender in India’s school textbooks: what the living paper finds in its national textbooks, in their words and in their pictures. Figures as of 12 November 2026.

Words

How often women and girls are mentioned in India’s 888 English national textbooks. Adjusted for subject, grade and book length (the paper’s model), India’s share is the fourth lowest of the 41 countries ranked.

  • 32.4%

    of gendered words refer to women and girls, adjusted for subject, grade and book length (95% interval 31.1–33.6).

  • 31.5%

    of gendered words in all its national textbooks refer to women and girls (pooled, no adjustment).

  • 888

    national textbooks scored.

By state

Each state’s female share of gendered words in India’s national textbooks, adjusted for subject, grade and book length.

Each dot is a state’s adjusted share and each line its 95% interval; books with no state form a group of their own. State figures cover each board’s own books; where a state board reprints NCERT books, those count once, as NCERT, and a book Andhra Pradesh and Telangana share counts once, as the 2024 paper’s Telangana copy. Most of the state books (364 of 659) are 2020–23 editions from Lee Crawfurd’s 2024 Ideas for India analysis, obtained through an unofficial mirror of the boards’ books (ncertbooks.guru). Chhattisgarh has books in one subject group only (Maths), so its figure assumes that the subject differences seen elsewhere in India hold there too.

Each state’s adjusted female share of gendered words, on a map.

Pictures

A model looked at 20 random pages of each of India’s 8,755 national textbooks, in every language, and recorded each person pictured.

  • 42.9%

    of the people pictured are women or girls (91,481 people).

  • 8.5%

    of the people shown as leaders are women (5,445 leaders).

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length. The pictures come from a model (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) that coded 20 random pages of each national textbook, in any language, following a written coding manual (pages a first look found no person on were not coded further); second copies of a book are counted once; the model’s disability flags are not yet checked by eye, so no disability figures are shown.
