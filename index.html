Key figures

  • 35.1%

    of gendered words in national textbooks refer to women and girls.

  • 2,974

    national textbooks: 1,210 from the 2024 paper and 1,347 found since.

  • 76

    countries, 42 of them added since the paper.

Across 2,974 national textbooks from 76 countries, 35.1% of gendered words refer to women and girls: 1.9 male words for every female word. Among the 41 countries with at least 10 national textbooks in the model, the adjusted share runs from 28.7% in Maldives (95% interval 21.2–36.2) to 69.0% in Zimbabwe (59.0–79.0); intervals are wide where books are few. Book by book, words about children are 38.6% female and words about adults 41.5% (a difference of −2.9 points, 95% interval −4.2 to −1.6). Among the 1,482 books published from 2000 on, the female share changes by −0.7 points a decade within countries (95% interval −4.3 to +3.0), so the data cannot rule out no change. Across countries, the adjusted female share shows no clear link with income (correlation −0.03).

Gendered words are nouns and pronouns such as she, mother and king; national textbooks are official English student textbooks. The figures apply the paper’s method to more books, so they differ from the published paper: see Methods.

Last updated 10 October 2026. The numbers are rebuilt as new books are scored.
