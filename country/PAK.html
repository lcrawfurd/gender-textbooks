Skin tone

The skin tone of the people pictured, judged by eye, in photos and in drawings and for women and men, Pakistan.

Each bar splits the people in a group whose skin tone could be judged into three bands, lighter, medium and darker, from left to right; the count after each group is how many people were judged, and the share in the darker band is at the right. The model judged tone by eye in a second pass over the pages with people. The 1,548 people whose tone could not be judged (black-and-white pictures among them) are left out. Photos and drawings are shown apart, since a drawing’s colours are the illustrator’s choice. Groups with fewer than 30 people judged are not shown. Pakistan, 367 national textbooks, 30 random pages each (10,984 pages, 2,135 with a picture); second copies of a book are counted once.

A second measure, from the colour of the skin pixels of each face (the individual typology angle, after Adukia et al. 2023), was possible for 1,389 people. It also finds the people in drawings lighter than those in photos (69.5% of 718 in drawings in the lighter band, against 37.9% of 535 in photos). Where both measures judged a face, they put it in the same band for 719 of 1,328 people (54.1%). It is not charted: shadows and faded or sepia photos darken faces, so it is unreliable at the darker end (see Methods).

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length. The pictures come from a model (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) that coded 30 random pages of each national textbook, in any language, following a written coding manual; second copies of a book are counted once, and every disability flag was checked by eye.
