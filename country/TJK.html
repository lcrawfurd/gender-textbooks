Jobs

The jobs people are shown in, in Tajikistan’s national textbooks. Women are 21.2% of the people shown in a job that can be matched to labour-market data, against 44.2% of the same jobs in Tajikistan’s labour market (ILOSTAT): a gap of −23.0 points (95% interval −34.2 to −2.5).

A person is listed when named, by at least two words (such as Kwame Nkrumah or Prophet Muhammad), in the body of at least 2 national textbooks; at most 10 are listed, and a list that would end inside a tie leaves out everyone with the last count. Names were found by a name finder (spaCy) in each book’s text, leaving out the first 8% and last 5% (forewords, author lists, references), and sorted by hand into real people and others (invented characters, the books’ own authors and officials, places); variants of one name count once. Names written only in a script other than the Latin alphabet are not found.

Pictures

A model looked at 20 random pages of each of Tajikistan’s 158 national textbooks, in every language, and recorded each person pictured.

  • 28.0%

    of the people pictured are women or girls (1,459 people).

  • 1.9%

    of the people shown as leaders are women (216 leaders).

How the figures are made

The words figures count gendered nouns and pronouns (she, mother, queen; he, father, king) in each national textbook, the method of the 2024 paper below, applied to the books found since; the adjusted share comes from a regression of each book’s female share on country, subject group, grade and book length. The pictures come from a model (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite) that coded 20 random pages of each national textbook, in any language, following a written coding manual (pages a first look found no person on were not coded further); second copies of a book are counted once; the model’s disability flags are not yet checked by eye, so no disability figures are shown.
