Key figures

  • 36.6%

    of gendered words in national textbooks refer to women and girls.

  • 2,369

    national textbooks: 1,247 from the 2024 paper and 1,122 found since.

  • 66

    countries, 32 of them added since the paper.

Across 2,369 national textbooks from 66 countries, 36.6% of gendered words refer to women and girls: 1.7 male words for every female word. Among the 43 countries with at least 10 national textbooks in the model, the adjusted share runs from 23.3% in Brunei Darussalam (95% interval 16.2–30.4) to 67.6% in Zimbabwe (56.8–78.4); intervals are wide where books are few. Book by book, words about children are 41.2% female and words about adults 43.2% (a difference of −2.0 points, 95% interval −3.6 to −0.4). Among the 1,401 books published from 2000 on, the female share changes by +1.2 points a decade within countries (95% interval −3.6 to +6.0), so the data cannot rule out no change. Across countries, the adjusted female share shows no clear link with income (correlation −0.22).

Gendered words are nouns and pronouns such as she, mother and king; national textbooks are official English student textbooks. The figures apply the paper’s method to more books, so they differ from the published paper: see Methods.

Last updated 5 October 2026. The numbers are rebuilt as new books are scored.
